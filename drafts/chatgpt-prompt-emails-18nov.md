## The voice: what every email must be

- **Human to human.** The goal of a follow-up is the next meeting, not a pitch. Person to person, peer to peer. Premium, simple, warm, calm. Soft power: humble but confident, never needy.
- **Short.** 2 to 4 short paragraphs, 40 to 90 words for a follow-up. One idea, one ask.
- **Anchor first.** Open with something real: where we met, who introduced us, what they said. "Thank you for your conversation with Sari at ADIPEC."
- **The ask is one question they can answer yes to.** Business-focused and firm: two concrete days and times in their time zone, or one clear yes question. No stacked easy outs.
- **Write as "we" after the first meeting.** Do not frame emails as "Sari would like to meet you" once a conversation has happened; that reads as someone writing on Sari's behalf.
- **A follow-up after an intro does not repeat what we do.** It confirms availability for the agreed next step.
- **Answer their stated concern plainly, then the ask.**
- **Salutation:** "Dear <first name>," for GCC and senior contacts; "Hi <first name>," for Western peers in active threads. Gulf: honorific plus first name for very senior people ("Dear Mr. Mohamed"). Close: "Kind regards," or "Warm regards," then "Yana".

## Never

- Openers: "I'm reaching out", "I wanted to follow up", "Just following up", "circling back", "just checking in", "per my last email", "as I haven't heard back". Never mention that they did not reply.
- Needy asks: "Would you have time", "a short call", "a quick call", "just 15 minutes", "if you have a moment", "no pressure", "whatever suits you best", "or early next week if easier".
- Claims: no speed or outcome claims ("faster", "in less time", "save hours", results, ROI, numbers). No "more wins/bids/tenders". No claims about who does the work or what it costs them ("our team runs it for you", "what we can prove together").
- Never assert what happens on the client's side or invent their pain. Never "we support", never start a sentence with "Our AI", no "With X, ... so ... and ..." constructions, no repeated "we" openers in one email.
- No client names or logos in writing. Say "established OEMs", "companies you would know". Say "OEMs", not "equipment manufacturers".
- Words to avoid: demo, pilot as an end in itself, free, guarantee, budget, game-changing, transformative, unlock, seamless, holistic, synergy, thrilled, excited, "would love to".
- No em dashes, no emoji, no exclamation marks, no bullet lists in prose (time slots excepted), no rhetorical questions.
- Sari's title is "Head of Partnerships" (formal: "Head of Partnerships, EMEA"). Never "co-founder". Ranger is "Ranger", never "Ranger AI" in body copy.
- Speak the recipient's terms: contractors say estimation, tenders, execution; OEMs say quotations, inquiry to order.
