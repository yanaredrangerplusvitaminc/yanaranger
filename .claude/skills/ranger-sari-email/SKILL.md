# Sari's Email Voice | Ranger

Write as a commercially experienced founder talking directly to another serious person. Sari's emails are warmer and more practical than his LinkedIn thought leadership. Advance the relationship; do not deliver a brochure in the inbox.

## Source-of-truth order

1. Explicit instructions for the current message and the recipient's actual conversation.
2. Sari's latest edits or directly authored emails, especially the 13 October 2026 review of AI Everything emails.
3. Prior correspondence with that recipient and trustworthy call or meeting transcripts.
4. Approved Ranger positioning and Sari editorial voice documents.
5. Generic examples only when nothing stronger exists.

Where connected, look at the live Google Doc, relevant Gmail thread, Granola notes, Slack instructions, and HubSpot/Monaco contact. Do not assume that a deck discussion made all lead follow-ups 'from Sari'; establish sender separately. The user's current instruction takes precedence over generic templates. If documentation is insufficient, keep the email honest and avoid fabricating familiarity, meetings, client cases, or promises.

## The email voice

- **Warm, serious and personal.** For a first email to senior GCC executives use 'Dear Mr. [first name]' (two recipients: 'Dear Mr. Mohamed and Mr. Atul,'). Use 'Dear [first name]' where Sari already writes that way to the person; 'Hi [name]' when already in an active thread. Spell names as the person's official or org profile shows them (Mohamed, not Muhammad, when that is how he is listed). Sari writes 'It was good to connect with you' and 'Thanks again', not ornate gratitude.
- **Name why this is relevant to them.** Lead with the conversation or concrete commercial context. Put their clients, teams, priorities and outcomes ahead of Ranger's credentials.
- **Describe delivery, not just the technology.** Ranger combines industry expertise, an AI platform and accountable execution. Product is the means to industrial results, not the object of the pitch.
- **Use ordinary wording.** Prefer 'show you how Ranger delivers value in practice' to slogans. Short paragraphs and a natural mix of sentence lengths work better than perfect symmetry.
- **Offer a credible, specific next move.** Sari changed a generic availability request to 'Do you have any availability on October 20 or 21? We will arrange the session accordingly.' Use actual known dates only; never invent availability or silently reuse past dates.
- **Keep the ambition proportionate.** A first conversation should earn a practical demonstration, client use-case discussion or focused POC, not pretend the client agreed to a deal.
- **Partnership language is concrete.** Choose 'collaboration' when appropriate rather than repeating 'partnership'. For IFS, a current relevant offshore-client example made the invitation more specific; do not name a confidential client without approval.
- **Make economic value explicit for channel partners.** A prospective distributor should see incremental sales in a defined industrial niche. Do not imply Ranger is asking them for investment; make clear Ranger delivers the specialist capability.
- **One clear ask, no hard sell.** Meet at their office if relevant, offer to coordinate dates, and avoid SDR-style demands or artificial scarcity.

## Executive meeting-request emails (rules from Sari's 15 October 2026 edits)

These are fixed rules for emails that propose a session with a senior client team. See references/sari-edits.md, 'Emdad', for the before and after.

1. **Open on the conversation, in one line.** 'Thank you for your discussion with Sari today.' Use 'discussion', not 'time' or 'call'. No self-introduction from the sender; the signature carries who they are.
2. **Name the Ranger team by role, Sari first.** 'led by Sari Saadi, along with our AI Solutions Director, Ibraheem Barbahan.' Sari needs no title when the email is about his own relationship; a colleague's title goes before the name.
3. **Say what the session does, in the client's work.** Name the activity: 'map (high level) your organization's Techno-Commercial flows, and identify where AI can create the most value across your organization.' Never a vague 'align on use cases'.
4. **Own the category.** Write 'Techno-Commercial' (capitalised, hyphenated) when describing the client's flows and their AI planning. It anchors Ranger's category in the client's own language.
5. **'Your organization', not the company name repeated.** Keeps it personal and avoids brochure tone.
6. **Service posture on the follow-up.** 'We will then return to you with a first roadmap of priorities, timeline and investment phases.' The roadmap comes after the session; never imply it is built in the room.
7. **Times as options, confirmed against Sari's calendar.** Up to two slots per day on the days Sari named, written 'Wednesday 21 October, 9.30 or 15.00'. Ask 'Would one of these times suit you?' and close with 'If another time works better, we will gladly adjust.'
8. **Subject in Sari's pattern.** 'Ranger & Emdad | AI strategy session': company pair, vertical bar, short executive label.
9. **Sign-off.** 'Warm regards,' then the sender's signature. If the email has no signature block, sign full name and title on a first email.
10. **Always verify names online before sending.** Check every recipient's name spelling, title and company on the internet (the company website, LinkedIn or the org profile) and use the spelling shown there. Do not rely on a call transcript, a spoken spelling or CRM alone. If no public source confirms it, flag it to the user before sending.
11. **Let Sari edit before it goes.** For first emails to VIP accounts, put the draft in a shared doc for Sari, then send or schedule only after his edits.

## Construction

1. Determine sender, recipient and relationship status.
2. Identify the **one** desired outcome for this email (book a session; revive a conversation; discuss a client; agree to look at a partner case).
3. Open with a real shared context: event, introduction, meeting, or prior discussion. Do not claim an in-person meeting unless established.
4. Explain what is relevant for the recipient in one short paragraph, with one concrete use case when approved.
5. Ask for a practical next step. Offer specific, confirmed dates when possible; otherwise invite availability.
6. Sign 'Sari Saadi / Co-Founder, Ranger' when Sari sends. When Yana sends on Sari's behalf, close 'Warm regards,' and let her signature carry name and title.
7. Check addresses, CC/BCC, meeting dates, title, deliverable claims and attachments. Treat the approved Stage 1 introduction deck as OPTIONAL unless Sari directed otherwise. Do not claim an attachment is included when it is not.

## Avoid

- Obvious rule-of-three sequences ('our process, methodology and impact', or three evenly balanced benefits); vary rhythm instead.
- Generic openings ('I hope this email finds you well', 'In today's AI landscape') unless natural in a specific relationship.
- Stock sales phrases ('exciting synergy', 'unlock transformational value', 'I would love to pick your brain', 'comparing notes').
- Long background on Ranger's platform, feature inventories, positioning speeches or a LinkedIn thought-leadership tone.
- Repeated formulaic introductions and closings across several recipients; each message should stand on its own.
- Em dashes, emojis, exclamation marks and inflated claims.
- Assumed verified emails, guessed domains, guessed surnames, invented POC scopes, or implied NDA approval.
- Accidental sender confusion: an email drafted for Sari is not necessarily to be sent by Yana; Sari chooses whether to copy or BCC her.

## Before returning a draft

Check: Does this sound like a founder who understands industry? Is the recipient's benefit obvious? Is there one natural next step? Is every name/client assertion grounded? Does it avoid a conspicuous three-part structure? Do date and recipient addresses have real evidence? Do not send, attach or CC anyone without an explicit request.
