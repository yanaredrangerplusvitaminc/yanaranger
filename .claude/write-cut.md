# Write, Cut: the editing bible

Sources:
- "Пиши, сокращай" (Write, Cut), Maxim Ilyakhov and Lyudmila Sarycheva: informational style (инфостиль).
- "The Elements of Style", William Strunk Jr. and E. B. White: the American classic behind the same idea, rule "Omit needless words".

Strunk and White, the sentence to remember:
> "Vigorous writing is concise. A sentence should contain no unnecessary words, a paragraph no unnecessary sentences, for the same reason that a drawing should have no unnecessary lines and a machine no unnecessary parts. This requires not that the writer make all his sentences short, or that he avoid all detail and treat his subjects only in outline, but that every word tell."

**Cutting is not stripping.** The goal is that every word tells, not that the text gets shorter. If a cut removes the meaning, the line stays. (Yana, 27 Oct, after a cut made the OTC post lose its sense.)

## Part 1. Ilyakhov: stop words and how to clear them

Stop words add volume, not meaning. Find them, then delete or replace them with a fact.

| Type | What it looks like | Do this | Ranger example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introductory and filler words | "actually", "in fact", "it is worth noting", "of course", "basically", "really" | Delete | "Where is AI actually paying off?" → "Where is AI paying off?" |
| Amplifiers | "very", "truly", "extremely", "highly", "incredibly" | Delete, or give the measure | "a very large tender" → "a 4,000-line tender" (only if sourced) |
| Evaluations | "great", "innovative", "leading", "unique", "powerful", "exciting" | Replace with the fact that lets the reader decide | "a leading AI partner" → "works with pump manufacturers and energy companies in four regions" |
| Generalisations | "always", "everyone", "all companies", "every client" | Narrow to what is true | "how we work with every client" → confirm, or "how we work" |
| Clichés | "in today's fast-moving world", "game-changing", "unlock", "seamless", "take it to the next level", "comparing notes" | Delete; say the specific thing | "We look forward to comparing notes" → cut |
| Bureaucratic phrasing (канцелярит) | "in order to", "with regard to", "for the purpose of", "carry out", "is engaged in" | Use the plain verb | "carries out the review of offers" → "reviews offers" |
| Nominalisations (verb turned into a noun) | "the implementation of", "the provision of", "make a decision" | Use the verb | "provides the automation of workflows" → "automates workflows" |
| Weak verbs and "to be" | "is", "are", "there is", "serves as", "acts as" | Use an action verb | "Ranger is a platform that connects…" → "Ranger connects…" |
| Passive voice | "decisions are made", "value is delivered" | Name who acts | "value is delivered to clients" → "our team delivers…" |
| Hedges and modals | "may help", "can potentially", "aims to", "strives to" | State what happens, or cut | "aims to help teams save time" → "gives teams hours back" (sourced) |
| Pleonasms | "past history", "future plans", "end result", "close proximity" | Keep one word | "end result" → "result" |
| Empty pronoun openers | "This is something that…", "It is important to…" | Start with the subject | "It is important to start with the work" → "Start with the work" |

### Ilyakhov's core principles

1. **Facts over evaluations.** Give the fact; let the reader draw the conclusion. An evaluation asks for trust; a fact earns it.
2. **Benefit to the reader.** Each paragraph answers the reader's silent question: "what is in it for me?"
3. **Honesty.** No exaggeration, no manipulation, no promises we cannot keep. Every claim can be traced.
4. **Respect.** No pressure, no begging, no flattery, no shouting. Do not explain what the reader already knows.
5. **Clarity of structure.** The main point first. One idea per paragraph. Short paragraphs. Lists only for real lists.
6. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the thing, the place, the number, the person.
7. **Write for one reader.** A senior person in industry, reading on a phone between meetings.

### The editing pass (Ilyakhov's method)

1. Read the draft once for meaning: what is this text for?
2. Mark every stop word from the table.
3. For each mark: delete, or replace with a fact. Never replace with another evaluation.
4. Read each sentence and ask: "What does it mean? What would the reader lose without it?"
5. Check the result still does its job (Ranger rule: go back to the goals and the sources if a cut removed something they need).

## Part 2. Strunk and White: the rules that matter for us

1. **Use the active voice.** "The team reviewed the bid" not "The bid was reviewed by the team".
2. **Put statements in positive form.** Say what is, not what is not. "He usually came late" beats "He was not very often on time." Ranger: "We start in the workflows" beats "We don't start with a knowledge base", unless the contrast is the point (Sari's own line keeps it).
3. **Use definite, specific, concrete language.** "A period of unfavorable weather set in" → "It rained every day for a week."
4. **Omit needless words.** Their examples:
   - "the question as to whether" → "whether"
   - "there is no doubt but that" → "no doubt"
   - "used for fuel purposes" → "used for fuel"
   - "he is a man who" → "he"
   - "in a hasty manner" → "hastily"
   - "this is a subject that" → "this subject"
   - "the reason why is that" → "because"
   - "owing to the fact that" → "since"
   - "in spite of the fact that" → "though"
   - "call your attention to the fact that" → "remind you"
5. **Keep related words together.** Do not split subject and verb with a long clause. The sentence "our work, with clients around the world, connects…" was hard to read for this reason.
6. **Place the emphatic words at the end.** The word you want remembered goes last: "…from inquiry all the way to cash."
7. **Express coordinate ideas in similar form.** Parallel ideas, parallel grammar.
8. **Avoid a succession of loose sentences.** Vary the rhythm; do not chain clauses with "and", "which", "while".
9. **Do not overstate. Do not affect a breezy manner. Prefer the standard to the offbeat.** Serious is never loud.

## Part 3. How this applies to Ranger posts

- One test per sentence: what does it mean, and what would the reader lose without it?
- A cut that removes meaning a goal needs is reversed: go back to the goals and to Sari's words.
- Long sentences are split where they carry two ideas, never by dropping the idea.
- Evaluations ("leading", "most useful") are allowed only when they are Sari's approved wording or when the fact behind them sits in the same sentence.
- Character comes from a true, specific detail that carries meaning, never from adjectives.

## Part 4. What makes copy read as machine-written (research, 27 Oct)

Yana: "People don't speak this way and they don't write this way." Sources: Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup, via TechCrunch 20 Nov 2025: https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/20/the-best-guide-to-spotting-ai-writing-comes-from-wikipedia/); Paralect, "Signs of AI slop in writing" (https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-writing); Westcliff University Writing Center, "Avoiding AI-Like Writing" (https://writingcenter.westcliff.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Avoiding-AI-Like-Writing.pdf); Pangram, "Comprehensive Guide to Spotting AI Writing Patterns" (https://www.pangram.com/blog/comprehensive-guide-to-spotting-ai-writing-patterns).

Tells, and what we do instead:
1. **Even rhythm.** Every sentence the same length and shape; every paragraph the same size. → Mix one short line with longer ones. Let a paragraph be one sentence.
2. **Balanced pairs.** "the engineers who design… and the sales teams who price…", "to X and to Y". → Keep one, or merge into a plain phrase.
3. **"Not X, but Y" contrasts and pivots** ("though", "but", "instead"). → State the thing directly.
4. **Covering every angle, committing to none.** → One point of view per post.
5. **No firsthand detail.** → One concrete, true detail beats three abstractions (sourced only).
6. **Summary closers and stock connectors** ("Overall", "moreover", "the same approach…"). → End on the last real point.
7. **Promotional words** ("valuable", "key", "powerful"). → Facts.
8. **Stiff register.** No contractions, formal links. → Write the way a senior person talks: contractions are fine, plain verbs.

A single tell is not proof; several together are. The fix is not swapping words, it is a clear point of view and real specifics.
