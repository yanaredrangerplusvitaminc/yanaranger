# Marketing intel

Signals from LinkedIn, competitors and the market, evaluated for Ranger marketing.

Rules
- One entry per signal, newest first.
- Every claim carries a source and a status: verified, secondary, or unverified.
- Third-party figures are context only. We never present them as Ranger results.
- Old entries are marked superseded, never deleted.

Status key
- **Verified**: checked against the primary source.
- **Secondary**: confirmed by a summary or transcript service, not the primary source.
- **Unverified**: only seen in the post that surfaced it.

## 2026-11-05. IFS Intelligence: "understands your operations, not just your documents"

**Source**: [IFS Intelligence product page](https://www.ifs.com/en/products/ai/ifs-intelligence), shared by Yumna in #ranger-gtm on 4 Nov 2026. Page capture dated 5 Nov 2026, provided by Yana.
**Verdict**: watch and position against it. Adjacent, not head-on, but their headline aims at document AI.

### What was said

All claims below are verified against the page capture. They are IFS marketing claims, not tested capability.

1. **Positioning.** "Industrial AI at the point of work." The hero line: "IFS Intelligence understands your operations, not just your documents."
2. **Scope.** One intelligence layer across orders, production, logistics and maintenance, grounded in data already inside IFS (their ERP, asset and field service software).
3. **Three modes.** Cowork to ask and act, Loops for AI agents that work within rules, such as order expediting and maintenance scheduling, and Runway to build customisations.
4. **Governance.** An "Industrial AI Harness" with identity, approvals, risk and audit. The AI prepares a change and a named person approves or declines it, with a full audit trail.
5. **Engineer-to-order.** Named under "Invest": spot a project slipping, prepare the change order, route it for approval.
6. **Availability.** Waitlist only. No pricing, no customer names on the page.

### Why it matters for Ranger

1. **Their headline is a jab at us.** "Not just your documents" frames document AI as the lesser category. Buyers will hear this from a vendor they already pay.
2. **Different starting point.** IFS reasons over structured data already in the system. Our work starts earlier: the customer's inquiry, specs and tender pack, before anything exists in an ERP. The quote is built before the order is entered.
3. **Human-in-control is now table stakes.** Approvals and audit trails are their main message. "Humans decide" alone will not set us apart. Citing the exact page, and saying "not found", still can.
4. **ERP vendors are moving in.** Industrial buyers on IFS may expect AI to come bundled. Our case has to be the workflow IFS does not cover.

### How we could use it

1. **Positioning line.** "Your ERP knows what is in your system. Ranger reads what is not in it yet: the customer's spec."
2. **Objection handling.** When a buyer says "our ERP vendor is adding AI", ask where their inquiries and tender documents live before they reach the ERP.
3. **Account check.** Find which named accounts run IFS. They could be a risk or a fit, depending on whether we sit in front of their ERP.
4. **Partnership angle.** IFS runs a partner programme. A Ranger-to-IFS handoff, from quote to order, may be worth a conversation later.

### Cautions

1. **Not yet live.** It is a waitlist page, so claims are untested. We should not attack features buyers cannot see yet.
2. **No public comparison.** We position ourselves, we do not name IFS in external material.
3. **Watch the release.** Worth checking again when it reaches general availability, especially whether Loops reaches quoting or tendering.

## 2026-11-05. Petrofac: selective growth through disciplined bidding

**Source**: Oil & Gas Middle East cover story, shared by Sari in #ranger-gtm on 4 Nov 2026 as "a great way to describe one of the capabilities we support our clients with".
**Verdict**: use it. It names the tendering problem in the buyer's own words.

### What was said

1. **Headline.** Petrofac's Manish Bhojwani says selective growth and consistent execution will define the company's next phase. Status: verified for the headline only, from the cover image Sari shared.
2. **Theme.** The magazine describes the story as Petrofac resetting its growth strategy around disciplined bidding, governance and execution. Status: secondary ([Oil & Gas Middle East](https://www.oilandgasmiddleeast.com/)). Article body not found.
3. **Context.** Petrofac went through administration and a creditor-approved restructuring, including the sale of Asset Solutions. Status: secondary ([administration](https://www.oilandgasmiddleeast.com/news/petrofac-enters-administration), [CVA approved](https://www.oilandgasmiddleeast.com/news/petrofac-cva-approved)).

### Why it matters for Ranger

1. **The capability.** For an EPC (engineering, procurement and construction contractor), selective growth means bidding on fewer tenders and winning the right ones. That is our tendering workflow: read the tender pack fast, see the risk early, decide bid or no-bid, and respond consistently.
2. **Buyer language.** "Disciplined bidding" and "consistent execution" are how EPC leadership talks about the problem. We can use their words rather than ours.
3. **Gulf relevance.** Petrofac's core markets are the Middle East and North Africa. The theme fits our EMEA and Gulf focus after ADIPEC.
4. **Our clients bid into EPCs.** Suppliers like KES respond to EPC tenders, including Petrofac packages. Selectivity at the EPC raises the bar for every supplier bid. Internal only, not for external use.

### How we could use it

1. **Capability line.** One line for the tendering capability: "Bid on fewer tenders, win the right ones, and respond the same way every time."
2. **Thought leadership.** A post from Sari on selective growth in EPC: why bid discipline starts with reading the tender pack properly, and where AI that cites every page helps.
3. **Discovery question.** For EPC and supplier first calls: "How do you decide which tenders to pursue, and how long does that decision take?"

### Cautions

1. **Read the article first.** Only the headline is confirmed. We should read the full piece before quoting Bhojwani.
2. **Sensitive company.** Petrofac is coming out of administration. Reference the theme, not the restructuring, and never imply a relationship.
3. **Client confidentiality.** Our clients' work on Petrofac bids stays internal.
4. **Not in CRM.** Petrofac has no company record in HubSpot. Worth deciding whether it belongs on a named account tier.

## 2026-11-05. Palantir: "The atomic unit is the workflow"

**Source**: LinkedIn post shared by Yana, on Palantir's Q3 2026 earnings call (Monday 2 Nov 2026).
**Verdict**: use it. Strong fit with how we already sell.

### What was said

1. **Workflow, not worker.** Shyam Sankar, Palantir CTO: the enterprise unit is the workflow, not the knowledge worker. Productivity gains alone can show no ROI. Software development is the exception, because there the knowledge worker is the workflow. Status: secondary ([Yahoo Finance transcript](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PLTR/earnings/PLTR-Q3-2026-earnings_call-722806.html), [BigGo summary](https://finance.biggo.com/quote/PLTR/earnings-call/US_PLTR_2026-11-02), [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-what-palantir-technologies-pltr-said-on-its-q3-earnings-call-2026-11-02/)). Exact wording of the quotes in the post not confirmed.
2. **New deal shape.** Alex Karp, CEO: buyers now arrive saying what they have does not work and their IP is at risk. Faster to deployment, shorter first contract, then large expansion. Status: unverified.
3. **Expansion examples.** Home appliance company: about $1M pilot in Q2 to $15M a year in Q3, across commercial, finance and supply chain. Industrial materials company: nearly $3M pilot in Q2 to $29M a year in Q3. Status: unverified.
4. **Revenue mix.** Post says roughly 50/50 government and commercial. Q3 2026 revenue $2.313B, US commercial $984M. Status: verified for the totals ([8-K press release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001321655/000132165526000045/a2026q3ex991pressrelease.htm)). The 50/50 split is unverified.

### Why it matters for Ranger

1. **Same unit of value.** We already sell workflows: inquiry to order, order to remittance, tendering. A large, credible voice now says the market should buy this way. Borrowed authority for our framing.
2. **Same deal motion.** Short paid PoC on one workflow, expansion only if it works. This is our PoC scoping and commercial packaging stage. The Palantir examples show buyers accept a small first contract with a large second one.
3. **Same measurement.** "Stop counting logins, measure what the workflow produces" is our value evidence stage and RAM in one line. Quote turnaround, inquiries processed, hit rate, not seats or adoption.
4. **Industrial buyers named.** One of the two expansion examples is an industrial materials company. That is our buyer.

### How we could use it

1. **Thought leadership.** A post from Sari: the workflow is the unit, applied to industrial techno-commercial work. Cite the call, add our view on ITO.
2. **Discovery and PoC framing.** One line for decks and first calls: "We start with one workflow and measure what comes out of it."
3. **Objection handling.** When a buyer asks for a broad copilot rollout or seat pricing, this gives us a neutral, cited reason to narrow to one workflow.

### Cautions

1. **Quote the call, not the post.** Confirm the exact wording and the deal figures in a primary transcript before any external use.
2. **No comparison.** Palantir's deal sizes are not our benchmark. Use them to show the motion, never as expected Ranger numbers.
3. **Scale gap.** Palantir sends forward deployed engineers. Worth deciding how we describe our own delivery model so the parallel does not overpromise.
