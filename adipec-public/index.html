3. Where we focus

  • Halls 3 to 6 are the core. ADNOC, Shell, TotalEnergies, ExxonMobil, Aramco. Further out: EPCs and OEMs.
  • Tier 1. OEMs and engineer-to-order makers: pumps, turbines, engines, gears, compressors. We are already there and can repeat the same motion.
  • EPCs. At least one prime (Technip, Saipem, Técnicas Reunidas) and two or three smaller ones.
  • Oilfield services. Halliburton and similar.
  • Strategic. NOCs and operators: ADNOC, Aramco. Sari leads.
  • Tier 2. Simpler products: instruments, valves, gaskets, panels. Lower priority. Ibraheem can take these.
  • Shortlist. From day one we pick 4 to 7 companies with an executive sponsor and group-level potential. Suggest one in the Live chat and say why. Sari decides.
  • Skip procurement people. Everyone chases them. We want estimating, bidding, engineering and commercial.
  • Don't go booth by booth. Skip and loop back, so we don't look like we're selling.

4. Opening a conversation

  1. Give a reason to be there. "We work with leading companies on AI that takes processes from 2 days to 10 minutes."
  2. Talk like a colleague. "How's your bidding season going? A lot of projects are coming out from ADNOC and Aramco."
  3. Let them talk. Their products, why ADNOC chooses them, their best competitors. Invite friendly people for a coffee.
  4. Use what you heard. After 5 or 6 booths, mention a specific project, product line or competitor. It builds trust fast.
  5. Bridge. "Have you built any techno-commercial AI yet?" Then ask for their card, or who handles proposals or engineering.

Busy booth? Walk past and make eye contact. A day later, "I know you from somewhere" opens it.

5. Their words

Oil and gas people test outsiders with a precise commercial question, like "have you done any upstream onshore contracting in the last cycle?" A broad answer and they switch off. The right words, built on, and they ask "who do you work with?" A known name there books the meeting.

  • Say pre-award and post-award. Pre-award is the work to win the project, post-award the work to deliver it.
  • Don't say procurement (they'll send you to procurement) or only "bidding".
  • Speak their language: floating a tender, CapEx for upstream, BOQ, bid packages, deadlines.
  • Give 60 to 70% of the answer. Offer the rest in a 30-minute meeting.

In one line. Technical: "We help you capture, engineer, bid and deliver, from pre-award to post-award, as a team embedded with you." Commercial: "From the moment an inquiry is floated until it's cash in hand, we take care of the whole function, team and technology."

Who we work with. Start with "leading companies you'll know." If they push: Celeros, PumpWorks, and ADNOC, where we run two AI projects, one offshore and one onshore. Most clients are in the US and Europe, not only oil and gas. You don't have to answer everything: "our director can share more."

6. The 3 questions you'll hear most

"We already have Copilot."

  • Technical: Copilot is fine for general questions. On bid work it's inconsistent and weak on hard engineering inputs. We work inside your own templates, so the output comes back in your format.
  • Commercial: We're not one-size-fits-all. We're techno-commercial AI, a team plus technology, turnkey.
  • Next: "Happy to show you on one of your own inquiries."

"We're doing AI internally, or with Oracle or SAP."

  • Technical: ERP, CRM and CPQ each hold one part. The work between them, reading the inquiry, checking compliance, building the quote, is where we sit.
  • Commercial: AI built 6 to 18 months ago is already out of date. Our clients stay the most advanced because the team keeps improving it.
  • Next: "Let me show you in a real meeting rather than talk."

"How do you sell this? Who do you work with?"

  • Technical: We start with one workflow and test it on your live bids.
  • Commercial: See "Who we work with" above. Our clients will vouch for us.
  • Next: "Let me introduce you to our director."

7. The next step and bringing Sari in

  • Bring Sari in when a user-level champion is ready to take it to their executive sponsor, or the sponsor is in front of you and it feels right. Post in the Live chat: company, person and title, where, how long they're there, why now.
  • Let them name the next step. "We should definitely stay in touch. What would you like the next step to be? Should I bring our director over now, or set up a meeting after ADIPEC?" The yes comes from them.
  • Book it yourself if the person can bring in their sponsor.
  • Never "we'll get back to you". Lock a date while you're still there.
  • Don't burn it. If they aren't listening, step away and post it in Live so someone else can try later.
  • Sari's card only for warm, high-potential contacts.

Every conversation ends one of three ways: bring someone in now · promise to come back and post it in Notes · book the meeting.

9. Staying connected

  • Live chat: real time. Sari, a slot, a hand at a booth.
  • Notes chat: a short voice note after every conversation, before you move on. Cards and badge photos too. Voice beats typed shorthand.
  • Need context mid-conversation? Ask Yana in real time.
  • Sari runs a separate small chat for Team 1.

10. After the show

  • No emails during ADIPEC, and none the week after. We reach out in the second week. "I know the first week will be busy. Should I reach out the week after?"
  • LinkedIn on the spot with everyone you talk to. Your QR: LinkedIn app, search bar, QR icon, My code.
  • Sari sends a LinkedIn request, no note, to everyone he met, the same day.
  • Everything in Notes gets a personal follow-up from Yana by the next morning.

11. Demo

  • Know the modules well enough to show one part when the conversation leads there. No full demo. The technical team presents.
  • End with: "This isn't the right setting. Let's do a proper meeting, like we do with our other clients."
